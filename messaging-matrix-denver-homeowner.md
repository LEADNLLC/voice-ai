# Messaging matrix — Denver homeowner, high Xcel bill

Built the way the module describes: outside in. Everything below is in the
homeowner's language, not All Access's. Nothing here says "renewable energy program",
because that is your word for it, not theirs.

Use this as the source for openers, objection handling, and the reverse pitch. When
something in the script stops working, come back here rather than inventing new copy.

---

## The persona

Colorado homeowner, owns a single family home, Xcel customer, bill over about a hundred
a month. Filled in something online at some point, possibly months ago, possibly barely
remembers it. Gets called by solar companies constantly and is sick of it.

**Critically: they are not shopping for solar. They are annoyed about a bill.** That gap
is where most solar scripts die.

---

## 1. Priorities — what they actually care about

Not solar. Not panels. Not the environment, mostly. These:

- The power bill stops climbing every single year
- Knowing roughly what the bill will be so it can be budgeted
- Not getting taken advantage of by a contractor
- Not taking on debt or anything touching their credit
- Not having to think about it much

> In their words: *"I just want to know what I'm paying and have it stop going up."*

## 2. Current solutions — what they do about it today

- Pay it and complain about it
- Thermostat games, ceiling fans, unplugging things, telling the kids to shut the door
- Xcel budget billing, which smooths the bill but does not lower it
- Got a solar quote once, saw a loan and a twenty year commitment, backed away
- Assumed solar needs a big upfront payment they do not have

## 3. Problems — why those are not working

This is the section that goes in your talk track. Problem language converts roughly
three times better than product language.

| What they're doing | Why it fails them |
|---|---|
| Just paying it | Rates keep rising and there is no ceiling. Nothing they do makes next year cheaper. |
| Conserving harder | Saves a few dollars, costs daily effort, bill still goes up |
| Budget billing | Averages the pain, does not remove it. They still pay the same over a year. |
| Solar loan quotes | Debt, a credit pull, and a lien on the house. Feels like a bigger commitment than the problem. |
| Assuming it costs a fortune | The reason they never looked seriously, and it is not true for a PPA |

**The one sentence version:** they are locked into a price they do not control, and the
only fix anyone has offered them came with a loan attached.

## 4. Aspirations — what good looks like in 12 months

- The rate is locked, so the bill is predictable
- Paying less than they are now, not more
- No loan, no credit pull, no lien
- It was easy and nobody pressured them
- They tell a neighbor about it

## Reverse pitch options

Your current TURN 3 is a **push** frame: it leads with the program. That is fine and it
has been testing OK. These are **pull** frames that lead with their world instead. Worth
A/B testing against what you have rather than swapping blindly.

**A — the rate increase (most direct)**
> "so I got your info here... honestly the reason I'm callin', most folks 'round here
> have watched their Xcel bill climb every year and there's nothin' they can really do
> about it. That's the thing we fix. You still livin' at {{address}}, correct?"

**B — the loan objection, pre-handled**
> "so I got your info here... most people I talk to looked at solar once, saw a loan and
> a twenty year thing, and backed right off. This isn't that, there's no loan at all.
> You still livin' at {{address}}, correct?"

**C — social proof, local**
> "so I got your info here... we've been workin' with a lotta folks around Denver who
> were just tired of Xcel goin' up every year. You still livin' at {{address}}, correct?"

**B is the one I would test first.** It disarms the exact objection that killed the Amy
call before she can raise it, and "there's no loan at all" is the single most valuable
sentence you have with this audience.

## Objection language, sourced from the matrix

Reframe every objection back to a priority, never to a product feature.

| They say | Weak answer (product) | Strong answer (their world) |
|---|---|---|
| "Not interested" | "It's a great program" | "totally fair. Just curious, is it the solar part, or have ya just been burned by one of these calls before?" |
| "Too expensive" | "It's affordable" | "there's nothin' to buy. No loan, no money out of pocket. That's usually the part people don't believe." |
| "I need to think" | "What questions do you have?" | "sure. Is it the commitment part, or just not a great time?" |
| "Already looked at solar" | "Ours is different" | "yeah, was it one of those where they wanted ya to finance the panels? 'Cause that's the part most people didn't like." |
| "Send me info" | "Sure, what's your email" | "I can, but every roof and bill is different so it'd just be a generic PDF. Fifteen minutes at the house gets ya your actual numbers." |

## What NOT to say

Words that belong to you, not to them. Every one of these is a buzzword to a homeowner:

`renewable energy program` · `PPA` · `power purchase agreement` · `energy independence` ·
`going green` · `sustainable` · `clean energy solution` · `savings potential` ·
`consultation` · `qualify you`

Say `your bill`, `Xcel goin' up`, `lockin' in your rate`, `no loan`, `no money out of
pocket`, `fifteen minutes`, `someone comes out`.

## How to keep this current

The module says to mine real customer language. You are now generating exactly that
every hour — your Retell transcripts.

Once you have thirty or forty connected calls, pull the transcripts and read what the
**homeowners** actually said. Not what Hailey said. Their words for the problem are
better than mine and better than yours, because they are the ones with the bill.

Then update this matrix and the script from it. That is the loop that keeps outbound
messaging alive instead of decaying into buzzwords.
